When to Amend

Common Reasons to Amend a Return

If any of these apply to you, it may be worth a second look:

Missed deductions or credits

Business expenses, education credits, retirement contributions, or other deductions you qualified for but didn't claim.

Corrected tax forms

A corrected W-2, 1099 or K-1 that arrived after you filed — common with brokerage statements and partnership K-1s.

Wrong filing status or dependents

Filing status or dependents that should have been claimed differently, changing your tax or your credits.

Business return errors

Mistakes on an S-Corp or partnership return that flowed through to the owners' personal returns.

Income that was left off

Correcting unreported income yourself — before the IRS finds it — generally means less in penalties and interest.

A preparer's mistake

Errors made by a previous preparer. We review the full return, not just the line in question.

Time Limits

Don't Wait Too Long

To claim a refund, you generally must file an amended return within three years of the date you filed the original return, or within two years of the date you paid the tax — whichever is later. A return filed before the April deadline is treated as filed on the deadline.

After that window closes, the refund is lost even if you were entitled to it. That's why it pays to review recent returns sooner rather than later.

Not every mistake needs an amendment. The IRS fixes simple math errors on its own and will usually write to you if a form was missing. We'll tell you when an amendment is actually worth filing.

What Gets Filed

How Amendments Work

Different returns are corrected in different ways. We handle each one:

ReturnHow it's corrected
Individual (Form 1040)Form 1040-X, with the changed schedules
S Corporation (Form 1120-S)An amended 1120-S, with corrected K-1s for shareholders
C Corporation (Form 1120)Form 1120-X
Partnership (Form 1065)Usually an Administrative Adjustment Request, with corrected K-1 information for partners
Connecticut (Form CT-1040)Form CT-1040X, when a change affects your Connecticut tax

The IRS says amended returns usually take 8 to 12 weeks to process, and sometimes up to 16. You can track the status with the IRS's "Where's My Amended Return?" tool.

Questions

Frequently Asked Questions

How far back can I amend a tax return?
To claim a refund, you generally have three years from when you filed the original return, or two years from when you paid the tax, whichever is later.
Will filing an amended return trigger an audit?
Amending a return doesn't by itself mean you'll be audited. Amended returns are routine, and an accurate amendment with supporting documents is the right way to correct a mistake.
What if I owe more tax after amending?
Then it's best to file and pay as soon as possible. Interest runs from the original due date, so correcting it sooner keeps the cost down.
Do I need to amend my state return too?
Usually. If a federal change affects your state tax, most states — including Connecticut — expect you to file a state amendment as well. We prepare both.
My previous preparer made the mistake. Can you still fix it?
Yes. We'll review the whole return, correct what's wrong, and make sure the same issue isn't repeated in the years since.
