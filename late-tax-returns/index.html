Why Act Now

Waiting Makes It More Expensive

Unfiled returns don't go away on their own. Here's what happens while they sit:

Penalties keep growing

The failure-to-file penalty is 5% of the unpaid tax for each month a return is late, up to 25%. A separate failure-to-pay penalty and interest also build up. Filing — even if you can't pay yet — stops the larger penalty from growing.

Refunds expire

If you were owed a refund, you generally have three years from the original due date to claim it. After that, the money is gone for good.

The IRS can file for you

The IRS can prepare a "substitute for return" using the income reported to it — without your deductions, credits, or business expenses. That usually means a much bigger bill than if you had filed yourself.

Business returns are penalized even with no tax due

Late S-Corp and partnership returns are penalized per owner, per month — even when the business owes nothing.

Good to Know

How Far Back Do You Need to Go?

The IRS generally expects the last six years of returns to be filed for you to be considered in good standing. That's often the place to start — though every situation is different, and we'll look at what makes sense for yours.

If you have a balance you can't pay all at once, the IRS offers payment plans. And if you have a clean history, you may qualify for First-Time Abatement, which can remove certain penalties for a single tax year. We'll tell you which options apply to you.

State returns matter too. Connecticut and most other states charge their own late-filing penalties and interest, so we prepare your state returns for the same years.

Questions

Frequently Asked Questions

I can't afford to pay what I owe. Should I still file?
Yes. The failure-to-file penalty is ten times larger per month than the failure-to-pay penalty, so filing on its own cuts how fast your balance grows. The IRS offers payment plans for balances you can't pay right away.
How many years of back taxes do I need to file?
The IRS generally looks for the last six years to be filed for you to be in good standing. We'll review your transcripts and recommend what makes sense for your situation.
I lost my W-2s and 1099s. Can you still file my returns?
Usually, yes. With your authorization we can get your IRS wage and income transcripts, which show the income reported to the IRS for each year.
Can IRS penalties be removed?
Sometimes. First-Time Abatement can remove certain penalties for one tax year if you have a clean compliance history. Penalties can also be reduced for reasonable cause, such as a serious illness. We'll tell you whether you may qualify.
Do I need to file my state returns too?
Usually. Connecticut and most other states have their own filing requirements, penalties and interest. We prepare the state returns alongside your federal returns.
