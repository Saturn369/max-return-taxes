Deadlines

Business Tax Deadlines

For calendar-year businesses. Missing these triggers penalties — for S-Corps and partnerships, even when no tax is owed.

ReturnBusiness typeDue dateExtended deadline
Form 1120-SS CorporationMarch 15September 15 (Form 7004)
Form 1065Partnership / multi-member LLCMarch 15September 15 (Form 7004)
Form 1120C CorporationApril 15October 15 (Form 7004)
Schedule C (Form 1040)Sole proprietor / single-member LLCApril 15October 15 (Form 4868)

An extension gives you more time to file, not more time to pay — tax owed is still due by the original deadline. When a deadline falls on a weekend or holiday, it moves to the next business day.

Maximize Your Return

Deductions We Make Sure You Don't Miss

Small businesses leave money on the table every year. We look for every one of these that applies to you:

  • Home office deduction for space used regularly and exclusively for business
  • Vehicle expenses — standard mileage or actual costs, whichever is better
  • Equipment and property depreciation, including Section 179 expensing
  • Self-employed health insurance premiums
  • Retirement contributions — SEP-IRA, Solo 401(k) and SIMPLE plans
  • The qualified business income (QBI) deduction for pass-through owners
  • Startup and organizational costs for new businesses
  • Business use of your phone, internet and software
  • Professional fees, insurance, and business interest
  • Connecticut's elective pass-through entity tax, where it helps

Questions

Frequently Asked Questions

When is my S-Corp tax return due?
For calendar-year S-Corps, Form 1120-S is due March 15. Filing Form 7004 extends the deadline six months, to September 15. A late S-Corp return is penalized per shareholder, per month — even if the business owes no tax.
Can you prepare my state return if my business isn't in Connecticut?
Yes. We prepare your federal return along with the state returns for every state where your business is required to file.
My bookkeeping is behind. Can you still help?
Yes. Send what you have — bank statements, receipts, any reports from your accounting software — and we'll tell you what's missing and how to get your records in shape to file.
Should my LLC elect to be taxed as an S-Corp?
It depends on your profit, what a reasonable salary for your role would be, and your state. For some owners it lowers self-employment tax; for others the added payroll costs outweigh the savings. We can run the numbers for your situation.
Do I have to meet with you in person?
No. Most clients work with us entirely remotely — documents are exchanged securely online and we talk by phone or video.
