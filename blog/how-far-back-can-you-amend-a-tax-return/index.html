How Far Back Can You Amend a Tax Return?

November 17, 2026·3 min read·Max Return Taxes

Found a mistake on a past return — or realized you missed a deduction? You may be able to fix it and get money back. But there's a deadline, and once it passes, a refund you were entitled to is gone for good.

The time limit for claiming a refund

To claim a refund, you generally must file an amended return within three years of the date you filed the original return, or within two years of the date you paid the tax — whichever is later.

One detail matters a lot: a return filed before the April deadline is treated as if it were filed on the deadline. So for a return filed early, the three years run from the April due date, not the day you actually filed.

If an amendment means you owe more tax, there's no reason to wait. Interest runs from the original due date, so the sooner you correct it, the less it costs.

Which form do you use?

Original returnHow it's amended
Individual (Form 1040)Form 1040-X
S Corporation (Form 1120-S)An amended 1120-S, with corrected K-1s for shareholders
C Corporation (Form 1120)Form 1120-X
Partnership (Form 1065)Usually an Administrative Adjustment Request, with corrected K-1 information
Connecticut (Form CT-1040)Form CT-1040X

Form 1040-X can be filed electronically for recent tax years, which is faster and easier to track than paper.

Amended Returns

Good reasons to amend

  • You missed deductions or credits you qualified for
  • You received a corrected W-2, 1099 or K-1 after filing
  • Your filing status or dependents were wrong
  • Income was left off the return
  • An error on an S-Corp or partnership return flowed through to your personal return

When you don't need to amend

The IRS corrects simple math errors on its own, and if you forgot to attach a form or schedule, it will usually write and ask for it. In those cases, an amended return isn't needed.

How long does an amended return take?

The IRS says amended returns generally take 8 to 12 weeks to process, and sometimes up to 16. You can follow the status with the IRS's "Where's My Amended Return?" tool, though it can take a few weeks after filing for a return to show up there.

Don't forget your state return

If a federal change affects your state tax, most states — including Connecticut — expect you to amend your state return too.
