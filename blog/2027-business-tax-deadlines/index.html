2027 Business Tax Deadlines: What's Due and When

November 17, 2026·3 min read·Max Return Taxes

Missing a business tax deadline is one of the most avoidable ways to pay a penalty — and for S-Corps and partnerships, a late return is penalized even if the business owes no tax. Here are the key federal dates for calendar-year businesses in 2027, covering tax year 2026 returns and 2027 estimated payments.

2027 federal deadline calendar

DateWhat's due
Friday, January 154th-quarter 2026 estimated tax payment
Monday, February 1W-2s to employees and the SSA; 1099-NEC to contractors and the IRS (moved from January 31, a Sunday)
Monday, March 15S-Corp (Form 1120-S) and partnership (Form 1065) returns, or Form 7004 extension; K-1s to owners
Thursday, April 15Individual returns including Schedule C (Form 1040), or Form 4868 extension; C-Corp returns (Form 1120), or Form 7004 extension; 1st-quarter 2027 estimated tax; last day for most 2026 IRA and HSA contributions
Tuesday, June 152nd-quarter 2027 estimated tax payment
Wednesday, September 15Extended S-Corp and partnership returns; 3rd-quarter 2027 estimated tax payment
Friday, October 15Extended individual and C-Corp returns
Tuesday, January 18, 20284th-quarter 2027 estimated tax payment (moved from January 15, a Saturday; January 17 is a federal holiday)

These are for businesses on a calendar year. Fiscal-year businesses have different dates. The IRS also regularly postpones deadlines for areas hit by federally declared disasters, so check for relief if you're affected.

Extensions: more time to file, not to pay

Form 7004 gives businesses an automatic six-month extension to file, and Form 4868 does the same for individuals, including sole proprietors. But tax owed is still due by the original deadline. Interest and the failure-to-pay penalty apply to anything paid late.

Why S-Corp and partnership deadlines matter so much

A late 1120-S or 1065 is penalized per shareholder or partner, per month, for up to 12 months — even when the business owes nothing. Those returns also produce the K-1s owners need for their personal returns, so filing late can hold up everyone's taxes.

Don't forget state deadlines

State due dates often follow the federal ones, but not always. Connecticut and other states have their own returns, extension rules and estimated payment schedules, so check every state where your business files.
