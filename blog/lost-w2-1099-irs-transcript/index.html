Lost Your W-2 or 1099? How to Get Your Income Records From the IRS

November 17, 2026·3 min read·Max Return Taxes

Missing tax forms are one of the biggest reasons people put off filing — especially when they're catching up on past years. The good news is that the IRS already has copies of most of them, and you can get that information yourself.

Use an IRS wage and income transcript

Employers, banks, brokers and other payers send copies of your W-2s, 1099s and similar forms to the IRS. A wage and income transcript shows the data from those forms for a given tax year, and it's available for up to ten years back.

Ways to get one:

  • Online: through your IRS Individual Online Account at irs.gov, where you can view and download transcripts right away after verifying your identity.
  • By mail: by requesting a transcript with Form 4506-T.
  • Through a tax professional: with your written authorization, your preparer can request transcripts for you.

What a transcript shows — and what it doesn't

Transcripts list the income, withholding and payment information that was reported to the IRS. But they have limits:

  • They don't include state wage or withholding information. For that, check old pay stubs, contact the employer, or ask your state tax agency.
  • They only show what was reported. Cash income or work paid without a 1099 won't appear, but it still has to be reported.
  • Information for the most recent tax year may not be complete until later in the year.
  • They don't show your deductions and expenses. Business owners should rebuild those from bank statements, receipts and other records.

Late & Unfiled Returns

If this year's W-2 never arrived

  1. Ask the employer first. W-2s are due by the end of January. If yours hasn't arrived by mid-February, contact the employer and confirm your address.
  2. Contact the IRS if the employer doesn't respond by the end of February.
  3. File on time anyway. If the form still doesn't come, you can file using Form 4852, a substitute W-2, based on your final pay stub. If the real form later shows different numbers, you may need to amend.

The bottom line

Lost paperwork shouldn't stop you from filing. Between IRS transcripts and your own records, you can almost always rebuild what you need — and the sooner you file, the less penalties and interest add up.
