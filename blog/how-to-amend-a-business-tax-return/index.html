How to Amend a Business Tax Return: S-Corps, Partnerships and C-Corps

November 17, 2026·3 min read·Max Return Taxes

Business returns get things wrong too: a missed expense, an incorrect depreciation figure, a K-1 allocation that doesn't match the ownership. The fix depends on what kind of business you have. And because S-Corps and partnerships pass their income through to their owners, one mistake can affect several returns.

S Corporations (Form 1120-S)

An S-Corp corrects a filed return by filing an amended Form 1120-S, with the amended return box checked, and issuing corrected K-1s to every shareholder whose share changed.

Because S-Corps generally don't pay federal income tax themselves, the tax effect shows up on the shareholders' personal returns. Each affected shareholder will usually need to file a Form 1040-X. That's why S-Corp corrections should be handled carefully and communicated to every owner.

Partnerships and multi-member LLCs (Form 1065)

Partnerships work differently. Under the partnership audit rules in effect since 2018, most partnerships can't simply file an amended 1065. Instead, they file an Administrative Adjustment Request (AAR).

With an AAR, the changes often don't go back to the partners' original returns. In many cases they're reported to partners and taken into account on the partners' current-year returns, or the partnership itself may pay the resulting tax. Which path applies depends on whether the change increases or decreases tax, and on choices the partnership makes.

Some small partnerships, generally with 100 or fewer partners who are all individuals, certain corporations or estates, can elect out of these rules each year on their return. A partnership that elected out for the year in question can generally file an amended 1065 and send corrected K-1s the traditional way.

Found an error on your business return? We'll figure out the right way to correct it — amended return or AAR — and handle the owners' amendments too.

Amended Returns

C Corporations (Form 1120)

A C-Corp amends its return on Form 1120-X. Because the corporation pays its own tax, the correction generally stays at the corporate level. Shareholders are only affected if something like dividend reporting changes.

Time limits

Businesses face the same basic refund deadline as individuals. To claim a refund, an amended return or AAR generally must be filed within three years of when the original return was filed, or two years from when the tax was paid if later. Partnerships also can't file an AAR once the IRS has opened an audit of that year.

Don't forget the states

A federal correction usually means state corrections too: the business's own state returns (in Connecticut, for example, the CT-1120X for corporations) and the owners' state returns in each state where the business files. If the business made Connecticut's pass-through entity tax election, that may need correcting as well.

Before you amend

  • Find the root cause. If the error was in the books, it may repeat in later years. Fix it everywhere.
  • Talk to all the owners first. Corrected K-1s mean amended returns and possibly more tax for them.
  • Check later years. Errors in depreciation, basis or inventory often carry forward into later returns.

This article is general information, not tax or legal advice for your specific situation. Tax rules change and have exceptions — talk with a tax professional before acting on it.
