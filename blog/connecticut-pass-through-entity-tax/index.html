Connecticut's Pass-Through Entity Tax: Should Your Business Elect It?

November 17, 2026·3 min read·Max Return Taxes

If you own an S-Corp, partnership or multi-member LLC in Connecticut, you've probably heard of the pass-through entity tax, often called the PET or PE tax. It used to be required. Since the 2024 tax year it's been optional, which means every eligible business now has a decision to make.

What the pass-through entity tax is

Normally, an S-Corp or partnership doesn't pay income tax itself. The profit passes through to the owners, who pay Connecticut income tax on their personal returns. With the pass-through entity tax, the business pays Connecticut tax at the entity level on its income. The owners then get a credit on their personal Connecticut returns for their share of the tax the business paid.

Why a business would choose it: the federal deduction

The benefit is mostly on the federal side. Individuals who itemize can deduct only a limited amount of state and local taxes, the "SALT cap." But the IRS has said that state income taxes paid by a pass-through business are deductible by the business when it calculates its income. That deduction isn't subject to the individual SALT cap.

So for owners whose state taxes go over the cap, having the business pay the Connecticut tax can lower their federal taxable income.

Why it doesn't make sense for everyone

  • If you take the standard deduction, or your state and local taxes are under the cap anyway, there may be little or no federal benefit.
  • The SALT cap changed recently. A 2025 federal law raised the cap for many taxpayers, phasing back down at higher incomes. That changes the math for owners who were near the old limit.
  • The Connecticut credit may not fully offset the entity-level tax, so the federal savings need to be worth the difference.
  • Multiple owners have different situations. The election is made by the business, so it affects every owner, including some who may not benefit.
  • Nonresident owners need to consider how their home state treats the Connecticut credit.
  • It adds work: the business has to make the election, make the payments and give owners the information they need for their credits.

We'll run the numbers for your business and each owner, with and without the election, and tell you whether it's worth it.

How to decide

The only reliable way is to compare the two scenarios for every owner. You need each owner's federal itemized deductions and SALT situation, their Connecticut credit, any other states involved, and the business's expected income. The answer can change from year to year, so it's worth revisiting annually.

Timing matters

The election is made each year through Connecticut's online tax system, myconneCT, and comes with its own deadlines and estimated payment requirements. Deciding late in the year can limit your options, so the best time to plan is before year-end.
