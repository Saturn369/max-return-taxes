Do You Need to Send 1099s to Contractors? The 2027 Rules

November 17, 2026·3 min read·Max Return Taxes

If your business paid freelancers, contractors or other self-employed people this year, you may need to send them a Form 1099-NEC by early 2027. The rules changed recently, so even if you've done this for years, they're worth a fresh look.

The big change: $600 is now $2,000

For years, you had to file a 1099-NEC for any contractor you paid $600 or more during the year. A 2025 federal tax law raised that threshold to $2,000 for payments made in 2026 and later, with inflation adjustments starting after that. The same change applies to many payments reported on Form 1099-MISC, such as rent.

That means the forms due in early 2027, covering what you paid in 2026, are the first ones under the higher threshold. Paid a contractor $1,500 in 2026? You generally no longer need to send that person a 1099-NEC.

The contractor still has to report that income on their own return, whether or not they get a form.

Who gets a 1099-NEC

Generally, you need to file a 1099-NEC when all of these are true:

  • You paid someone who isn't your employee, such as a freelancer, independent contractor or consultant
  • You paid them for services in the course of your trade or business, not personal expenses
  • The person is an individual, partnership or LLC that isn't taxed as a corporation
  • You paid them at least the threshold amount during the year: $2,000 for 2026 payments

Common exceptions

  • Corporations. Payments to C-Corps and S-Corps generally don't need a 1099. Payments for legal services are the big exception: attorneys get a 1099 even if their firm is incorporated.
  • Card and app payments. If you paid by credit card, debit card or a payment app's business payment option, the payment company handles the reporting on Form 1099-K. Don't also send a 1099-NEC for those payments.
  • Employees. People on your payroll get a W-2, not a 1099.
  • Personal payments. Hiring someone to paint your own house isn't a business payment.

Get a W-9 before you pay

The easiest way to stay on top of 1099s is to collect a Form W-9 from every contractor before you pay them. It gives you their legal name, tax classification and taxpayer identification number. That tells you whether they need a 1099 and gives you everything you need to file one.

If a contractor won't give you a valid taxpayer ID, you may be required to withhold 24% backup withholding from their payments and send it to the IRS.

Deadlines and how to file

Form 1099-NEC is due to both the contractor and the IRS by January 31. In 2027, January 31 falls on a Sunday, so the deadline is Monday, February 1, 2027. Unlike most tax forms, there's no automatic extension.

If you're filing 10 or more information returns in total, including W-2s and other 1099s, you must file them electronically. The IRS's free IRIS portal lets businesses e-file 1099s directly.

Some states have their own 1099 filing requirements, and Connecticut is one of them in many cases. Check your state's rules too.

Penalties for late or missing 1099s

The IRS charges a penalty for each 1099 that's filed late, filed with wrong information or never filed. The amount grows the later you file, and it's much steeper if the IRS finds you intentionally ignored the requirement. The penalty dollar amounts are adjusted for inflation every year. Filing on time, even if you need to correct a form later, is always cheaper.

Contractor or employee?

A 1099 only works if the person really is an independent contractor. If you control how, when and where someone does the work, they may legally be an employee. In that case, payroll taxes and a W-2 apply instead. Misclassification can be expensive, so if you're unsure, it's worth getting advice before year-end.
